# Neural Networks (Elsevier, ISSN 0893-6080): fit, expectations and desk-rejection risks for a paper on constructive SLFNs with certified partial monotonicity

Research date: 2026-10-01. Access constraints: OpenReview (web and API), the Crossref REST API via shell and ScienceDirect full texts were partly blocked by the egress proxy; Crossref queries did work through the fetch tool, and ScienceDirect abstract pages did load. All article metadata below comes from Crossref / ScienceDirect / arXiv unless stated otherwise.

## Key question 1 — Recent (2022–2026) *Neural Networks* articles on monotonic / shape-constrained / certified / constructive networks: what they contain, length and structure

### Takeaway
The journal has published only a handful of papers directly on monotonic networks since 2022 (DIEN 2024, MonoKAN 2026), one on derivative/gradient priors without equations (GradINN 2026), and a steady stream of constructive/randomized networks (stochastic configuration networks, ELM/RVFL theory). The two monotonic papers both (i) include a monotonicity proof/certification argument, (ii) benchmark on the same small tabular suite the submission uses (COMPAS, Loan, Heart Disease, Auto MPG, Blog Feedback) against Min-Max, DLN, Crystal, COMET/Certified MNN, CMNN, LMN and XGBoost, and (iii) report mean ± sd over a few repetitions without formal statistical tests — so the submission's scope (eight tabular datasets, nested CV, Friedman/Nemenyi) is at or above the published bar in evaluation rigour, and its compactness (DIEN is 9 printed pages) is not an anomaly.

### Inferences
- Monotonic networks are a niche but accepted topic at *Neural Networks*: two acceptances in ~2.5 years, both with a proof of the monotonicity guarantee plus benchmarks on the Liu et al. 2020 / Sivaraman et al. 2020 tabular suite. A submission that uses the same benchmark names (COMPAS, Loan, Heart Disease) and the same baseline families (Min-Max, CMNN, LMN, XGBoost) will look "in-distribution" to the handling editor.
- Published monotonic papers at the journal used 3 repetitions and no significance tests; nested CV with Friedman/Nemenyi over eight datasets exceeds that bar, which should be stated explicitly in the paper as a strength.
- The constructive/randomized-network community (Wang's SCN group, ELM/RVFL theory) does publish in the journal, and those papers lead with universal-approximation / convergence theorems and small synthetic + industrial datasets — the same template as the submission (UA theorem + synthetic targets + tabular benchmarks).
- Length: DIEN occupies 9 printed pages; MonoKAN is 18 single-column preprint pages with 2 tables. A ~3,000-word body with 5 tables, 1 figure, 1 algorithm and proofs in the supplement is at the short end but consistent with these examples and with the journal's two-column layout (the project's own `main_journal.pdf` estimate of ~16 pages seems to refer to the elsarticle preprint layout; a 3,000-word two-column paper would be far shorter).
- Formal verification/branch-and-bound as a *topic* is rare in the journal; the certificate should be framed as a tool that delivers certified monotonicity (the MonoKAN framing), not as a verification contribution per se.

## Key question 2 — Official guidance: article types, sections, supplementary material, data/code, highlights, graphical abstract, AI declaration, review model, turnaround, scope statements

### Takeaway
*Neural Networks* runs single-anonymized review with at least two reviewers, offers "Articles" without a word limit and "Letters" capped at 2,500 words / 25 references, requires authors to choose one of five sections (the submission fits "Learning Systems" or "Mathematical and Computational Analysis"), mandates 3–5 highlights (≤85 characters each), a data-availability statement with repository deposit (Option C), a generative-AI declaration at the end of the manuscript, and reports median times of 9 days to first decision, 78 days review and 208 days submission-to-acceptance; a subscription (no-APC) route exists alongside USD 2,760 OA.

### Cited Findings
- Article types: "Articles" are original full-length papers with no specified word limit; "Letters" are "up to 2500 words" with an "abstract (no more than 100 words), and a maximum of 25 references"; "Reviews/Tutorials" require prior editor contact; "Letters to the Editor" must not exceed "2 formatted pages" — [Guide for Authors](https://www.sciencedirect.com/journal/neural-networks/publish/guide-for-authors)
- Review model: "Single anonymized review process" with a "minimum of two reviewers for an independent expert assessment" — [Guide for Authors](https://www.sciencedirect.com/journal/neural-networks/publish/guide-for-authors)
- Journal sections: authors must specify one of five — Cognitive Science, Neuroscience, Learning Systems, Mathematical and Computational Analysis, Engineering and Applications — [Guide for Authors](https://www.sciencedirect.com/journal/neural-networks/publish/guide-for-authors); [Aims and Scope](https://www.sciencedirect.com/journal/neural-networks/about/aims-and-scope)
- Supplementary material: "Supplementary material should be accurate and relevant to the research" and must be cited in the manuscript text; after submission, "supplementary files can only be added or replaced in the revision stage" — [Guide for Authors](https://www.sciencedirect.com/journal/neural-networks/publish/guide-for-authors)
- Research data: "Option C applies" — authors are "required to: Deposit your research data in a relevant data repository" and "Cite and link to this dataset in your article" — [Guide for Authors](https://www.sciencedirect.com/journal/neural-networks/publish/guide-for-authors)
- Highlights: "Submit highlights as a separate editable file" consisting of "3 to 5 bullet points, each a maximum of 85 characters, including spaces" — [Guide for Authors](https://www.sciencedirect.com/journal/neural-networks/publish/guide-for-authors)

### Inferences
- A ~3,000-word body exceeds the Letter cap (2,500 words, 25 references, 100-word abstract), so the paper should be submitted as a regular Article; there is no upper limit, and brevity itself is not penalised by policy.
- "Time to first decision 9 days" is a median that is dominated by desk decisions; a paper that survives the desk should expect roughly 2.5 months per review round and ~7 months to acceptance, consistent with MonoKAN's ~14-month preprint-to-journal interval.
- The data-availability rule (Option C) makes a Zenodo/GitHub deposit of the benchmark splits, results JSONL/CSV and code essentially mandatory; the submission already downloads benchmarks from Zenodo, so citing that record plus a DOI-minted release of the repository satisfies the rule.
- The AI-use declaration must be present at the end of the first submission; the repository's build pipeline (`make_journal.sh`) should emit it.
- Section choice: "Learning Systems" matches the empirical/algorithmic contribution; "Mathematical and Computational Analysis" matches the UA theorem and the sound certificate. Since the paper's main claims are algorithmic (certified training + DCV) with proofs in the supplement, "Learning Systems" seems the natural pick, but this is a judgment call, not a documented rule.

## Key question 3 — What reviewers criticise in papers of this kind (public reviews and editorial pieces)

### Takeaway
The only public peer reviews retrievable were the NeurIPS 2020 reviews of COMET (counterexample-guided monotonic networks), the closest methodological ancestor of the submission; the four reviewers converged on scalability (tiny models, cost growing with features, no complexity analysis), too few datasets (two), restriction to piecewise-linear activations, a certificate that is solved at prediction time without termination guarantees, missing pseudo-code, and a lack of explicit limitations. OpenReview pages for ICLR 2023/2024 monotonic papers could not be accessed.

### Cited Findings
- COMET (Sivaraman et al., NeurIPS 2020) official reviews: Reviewer 1 — scalability concerns because the "largest" model tested was "3-layer/384 parameters" and the "deepest" "6-layer/96 parameters"; lack of evaluation across model sizes; "Missing explicit discussion of limitations compared to prior approaches" — [NeurIPS review page](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2020/file/8ab70731b1553f17c11a3bbc87e0b605-Review.html)
- COMET Reviewer 2 — praised that "The combination of the 2 algorithms presented in the paper provides a formal guarantee of monotonicity" but objected that "Guaranteeing monotonicity requires solving an SMT instance at prediction time" with no termination guarantees; "One outlier...can affect the value of the model for all possible parameter values"; counterexample augmentation may cause "systematic drift away from the original training data" — [NeurIPS review page](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2020/file/8ab70731b1553f17c11a3bbc87e0b605-Review.html)
- COMET Reviewer 3 — "any activation function that is not piecewise-linear is likely not feasible"; "Computational cost...is fairly large" and "does not scale well with...more features"; no theoretical complexity analysis, timing results suggest "exponential complexity" — [NeurIPS review page](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2020/file/8ab70731b1553f17c11a3bbc87e0b605-Review.html)
- COMET Reviewer 4 — "pseudo-code" should be included for reproducibility; evaluation uses only "two datasets" rather than a comprehensive suite; encoding details missing; wants a "detailed theoretical characterization" such as worst-case counterexample bounds — [NeurIPS review page](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2020/file/8ab70731b1553f17c11a3bbc87e0b605-Review.html)
- The bar set by the two monotonic papers the journal did accept: MonoKAN compared against nine baselines (Isotonic, XGBoost, Crystal, DLN, Min-Max, Non-Neg-DNN, COMET, CMNN, LMN) on five standard datasets with mean ± sd over three runs, no significance tests, public code — [arXiv 2409.11078v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.11078v2); DIEN compared Min–Max, Crystal, DLN, Certified MNN and an unconstrained MLP on synthetic + real data with a monotonicity proof — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0893608023007311)
- Newer baselines a reviewer may expect to see discussed: Scalable Monotonic Neural Networks (ICLR 2024) — [ICLR PDF](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/96e35b532b4932a86cce8c929ff3f960-Paper-Conference.pdf); "Advancing Constrained Monotonic Neural Networks: Achieving Universal Approximation Beyond Bounded Activations" (ICML 2025) — [ICML poster](https://icml.cc/virtual/2025/poster/45293); MonoKAN itself (same journal, 2026) — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0893608025011591)
- The journal's own research-data policy (Option C: deposit and cite) and AI-declaration requirement are compliance items checked at submission — [Guide for Authors](https://www.sciencedirect.com/journal/neural-networks/publish/guide-for-authors)

### Inferences
Main desk-rejection / major-revision risks for the submission, mapped from the evidence above:
1. **"Why single hidden layer?"** Every accepted monotonic paper and all recent competitors are deep (DIEN, MonoKAN, CMNN, LMN, SMNN). A reviewer will ask whether the constructive SLFN + certificate scales to deeper models or larger feature counts; COMET was criticised exactly on model size and feature scaling. The paper needs an explicit scalability/limitations paragraph with certificate timing vs. number of monotone features and n*.
2. **Benchmark scale.** Eight small tabular datasets is more than DIEN/MonoKAN used, but the COMET reviews show "only two datasets" is a standard objection; the Blog Feedback (276 features) and Loan Defaulter datasets from Liu et al. 2020 are the de-facto "large" cases in this literature — the paper already uses Loan (subsampled to 20,000 rows; that must be declared) and could add a sentence on why Blog Feedback is omitted.
3. **Missing SOTA.** MonoKAN (same journal, 2026), SMNN (ICLR 2024) and Sartor et al. / the ICML 2025 universal-approximation CMNN extension are not among the compared baselines (CMNN, LMN, Min-Max, GBTs); at minimum they need to be cited and positioned, which the project's `NOTES_pre_submission.md` already flags for Sartor et al.
4. **Certificate soundness and cost at prediction time.** COMET's reviewers objected to a certificate solved at prediction time with no termination guarantee; the submission's branch-and-bound certificate is run once at training/selection time, which should be stated explicitly, together with worst-case behaviour (completeness, timeouts, the 3,600 s budget) and the rigorous interval re-check that the notes file already lists as pending.
5. **Statistical rigour is a differentiator, not a risk.** Nested CV + Friedman/Nemenyi/Holm/Wilcoxon exceeds the three-run mean ± sd standard of the accepted papers; presenting it compactly (CD diagram + one table) fits the co-authors' preference for brevity.
6. **Reproducibility compliance.** Pseudo-code (one algorithm is present), public code with a DOI, data deposit (Option C), highlights file, AI-use statement and CRediT are formal checks; missing any of them triggers an administrative return before review.
7. **Framing for the "Neural Networks" audience.** The journal's scope text is broad; the certified-partial-monotonicity angle (MonoKAN's keyword "Certified partial monotonic ANNs") and the UA theorem align with both "Learning Systems" and "Mathematical and Computational Analysis" readers, whereas a purely procurement/tabular-application framing would push the paper toward *EAAI/ESWA*-type venues.
