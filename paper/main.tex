\begin{abstract}
The probability of the positive class is often known to increase or decrease with some inputs. We train constructive
single-hidden-layer networks, grown one hidden unit at a time, to respect such partial monotonicity on the whole input
domain and to prove it. Earlier work constrained the sign of the average gain of each input; here the constraint holds at
every point. We show that sign constraints on the weights, the classical way to guarantee monotonicity, cannot
approximate simple monotone targets with one hidden layer, whereas networks that are monotone on the domain are
universal approximators. To find them, closed-form gain constraints are imposed at anchors that grow with the
counterexamples of an adversarial search, and the final network is checked by a sound branch-and-bound certificate,
exact in pre-activation space; if the check fails, a sign-constrained solution is returned, so every network is
certified. On seven synthetic targets with a known probability, the certified networks reduce the error of
sign-constrained ones by 30\%. On eight datasets, including the COMPAS, Heart Disease and Loan Defaulter benchmarks,
under nested cross-validation, they match unconstrained networks and constrained monotonic neural networks in MCC,
outperform Min-Max and Lipschitz monotonic networks, and are certified in every fold, whereas only about a third of the
unconstrained networks are monotone.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

In many classification problems the direction of the effect of some inputs is known in advance: the probability of
death after heart failure should not decrease as the ejection fraction falls, and the probability of default should not
decrease as the debt-to-income ratio grows. A model that violates such relations may score well on a test set, but its
predictions cannot be defended to a clinician or a regulator, and its violations tend to appear where data are scarce
\citep{Sill1997,Cano2019,Liu2020}. \emph{Partially monotonic} networks enforce the relation for the inputs whose
direction is known \citep{DanielsVelikova2010}. \emph{Soft} approaches penalise violations at sampled points and give no
guarantee elsewhere; \emph{structural} approaches are monotone by construction but fix a specialised architecture;
\emph{certified} approaches train an ordinary network and verify it (\cref{sec:related}).

Constructive networks \citep{Ash1989,Fahlman1990,KwokYeung1997,HuangChenSiew2006} grow the hidden layer until a validation
criterion stops improving. For a single hidden layer, the natural structural choice --- non-negative (sign-constrained)
weights --- is not universal for monotone functions \citep{DanielsVelikova2010,Gupta2016}. In earlier work we imposed
gain-sign constraints on constructive networks for regression \citep{Sa2026}: the gain of each input, estimated over the
training data, must have the sign given by domain knowledge. This fixes the sign of the average effect of each input, an
established measure of its global effect \citep{HardleStoker1989}, but not the sign at every point. Here we require
monotonicity on the whole domain and prove it for every network returned. The contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Expressiveness}: single-hidden-layer sigmoid networks that are monotone on the input box, with a positive
gain margin, are universal approximators of continuous partially monotone functions (\cref{thm:ua}); the obstruction met
by sign constraints lies in the parametrisation, not in the depth (\cref{fig:surfaces}).
\item \emph{Counterexample-guided training} (\cref{sec:cegis}): closed-form gain constraints at anchors that grow with the
counterexamples of an adversarial search, with a repair-and-fallback step that makes every returned network certified.
\item \emph{A sound certificate for single hidden layers} (\cref{sec:certificate}), exact in pre-activation space.
\item \emph{An evaluation} on synthetic targets with a known probability and eight real datasets under nested
cross-validation, against thirteen alternatives including Min-Max, constrained monotonic and Lipschitz monotonic networks.
\end{enumerate}
Proofs, derivations, settings and complete results are in the Supplementary Material.

\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

\emph{Monotonic networks.} Non-negative weights \citep{ArcherWang1993}, monotonicity hints \citep{SillAbuMostafa1996} and
min-max networks \citep{Sill1997} were the first approaches; \citet{DanielsVelikova2010} extended min-max networks to
partial monotonicity and showed that one hidden layer of non-negative weights is not universal. Later architectures
include lattices \citep{Gupta2016,You2017}, integral networks \citep{Wehenkel2019}, constrained activations
\citep{Runje2023,Sartor2025}, Lipschitz-bounded networks \citep{Nolte2023,Kitouni2023}, monotone embeddings
\citep{Zhao2024dien,KimLee2024} and spline-based Kolmogorov--Arnold networks \citep{PoloMolina2026kan}. All fix the
architecture in advance.

\emph{Certification.} \citet{Liu2020} certify ReLU networks by mixed-integer programming and raise a monotonicity penalty
until certification succeeds; COMET \citep{Sivaraman2020} adds SMT counterexamples to the training data and builds
monotone envelopes at prediction time; \citet{PoloMolina2026cert} certify derivative signs by covering the domain with
Lipschitz balls. Our training loop follows counterexample-guided inductive synthesis \citep{SolarLezama2006}, but
counterexamples become hard constraints of the optimisation, no penalty is tuned, and the certificate exploits the
structure of a single hidden layer with interval global optimisation \citep{HansenWalster2004,Moore2009}; see
\citet{Liu2021verif} for network verification in general.

\emph{Constructive networks.} Units are added one at a time with frozen \citep{Fahlman1990} or retrained weights
\citep{MaKhorasani2004}, or drawn at random with output weights in closed form \citep{HuangChenSiew2006,WangLi2017}.
Weight initialisation by linearisation with weight reuse (WILCAR) was proposed for regression and multiclass
classification \citep{Sa2022,Fontes2021} and combined with gain-sign constraints for regression \citep{Sa2026}. Selecting
the size on the data used to report performance biases the estimate \citep{VarmaSimon2006,CawleyTalbot2010}. None of
these works guarantees monotonicity.

\section{Problem setting}\label{sec:problem}

Inputs are min-max scaled on the training data, so that they lie in the box $\B=[0,1]^p$, and $y\in\{0,1\}$. A
single-hidden-layer feedforward network (SLFN) with $n$ sigmoid units models
\begin{equation}\label{eq:slfn}
p_\theta(x)=\sig\bigl(z_o(x)\bigr),\qquad z_o(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{n} v_j\,\sig\bigl(z_j(x)\bigr)+c,\qquad z_j(x)=w_j^\top x+b_j ,
\end{equation}
with $\theta=(W,b,v,c)$, $W\in\R^{n\times p}$ with rows $w_j^\top$, and $\sig(t)=1/(1+e^{-t})$; $p_\theta(x)$ estimates
$P(Y=1\mid x)$, where $Y=1$ is the positive class (the event of interest). Prior knowledge is a \emph{signal vector}
$s\in\{-1,0,+1\}^p$: $s_i=+1$ ($-1$) states that $P(Y=1\mid x)$ does not decrease (increase) with $x_i$, and $s_i=0$
leaves $x_i$ free; $\I=\{i:s_i\neq0\}$.

\begin{definition}[Partial monotonicity]\label{def:mono}
$f:\B\to\R$ is \emph{$s$-monotone on $\B$} if $s_i\bigl(f(x+te_i)-f(x)\bigr)\ge0$ for every $i\in\I$, $x\in\B$ and $t\ge0$
with $x+te_i\in\B$. For $p_\theta$ this holds if and only if the \emph{gains} $g_i=\partial p_\theta/\partial x_i$
satisfy $s_ig_i(x)\ge0$ for all $x\in\B$, $i\in\I$.
\end{definition}

\section{Method}\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Constructive networks}\label{sec:constructive}
A constructive method trains networks with $n=1,2,\dots$ units and stops when a validation criterion has not improved
for a number of steps (the patience). \emph{WILCAR} \citep{Sa2022,Fontes2021} initialises the first unit by linearising
the network around the mean input and \emph{reuses weights}: the network with $n$ units starts from the one with $n-1$
units plus a new unit. \emph{RIXM} re-initialises all weights at every size (Xavier-type Gaussian, \citealp{Glorot2010}).
\emph{ELM} \citep{Huang2006} draws the hidden layer at random and solves the output layer. Constrained variants are
denoted WILCAR-C, RIXM-C and ELM-C. WILCAR and RIXM minimise
\begin{equation}\label{eq:loss}
J(\theta)=-\frac1N\sum_{k=1}^{N}\bigl[y_k\log p_\theta(x_k)+(1-y_k)\log\bigl(1-p_\theta(x_k)\bigr)\bigr]
+\frac{\lambda_0}{N}\bigl(\|W\|_F^2+\|v\|_2^2\bigr),
\end{equation}
the negative log-posterior per sample under a Gaussian prior of precision $\lambda_0$. Unconstrained networks are trained
with L-BFGS \citep{LiuNocedal1989}; constrained ones start from that optimum and are solved by SLSQP \citep{Kraft1994},
both with analytic gradients.

\subsection{Gains and constraint families}\label{sec:gains}

\begin{lemma}[Gains]\label{lem:gain}
$g_i(x)=\sig'(z_o(x))\sum_{j}v_j\sig'(z_j(x))W_{ji}$. With $c_{ij}=s_iv_jW_{ji}$ and
$h_i(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}c_{ij}\,\sig'(z_j(x))$, $s_ig_i(x)=\sig'(z_o(x))\,h_i(x)$; since $\sig'>0$, $s_ig_i(x)\ge0\iff h_i(x)\ge0$.
\end{lemma}

Thus $h_i$, a weighted sum of positive bell-shaped functions $\sig'(z_j)$ with weights of either sign, carries the whole
sign information. With exact gradients of $g_i$ with respect to the weights (Supplementary \cref{S-sup:gradients}), each
constructive step solves $\min_\theta J(\theta)$ subject to one of
\begin{align}
\text{mean:}\quad & \frac{1}{|\A|}\sum_{a\in\A}s_i\,g_i(a)\ \ge\ \varepsilon_{m}, && i\in\I, \label{eq:mean}\\
\text{anchor:}\quad & s_i\,g_i(a)\ \ge\ \varepsilon_{p}, && i\in\I,\ a\in\A, \label{eq:point}\\
\text{sign:}\quad & c_{ij}=s_i\,v_j\,W_{ji}\ \ge\ 0, && i\in\I,\ j=1,\dots,n, \label{eq:sign}
\end{align}
where $\A\subset\B$ is a finite set of \emph{anchors}. The mean constraint, over the training inputs, is the constraint
of our earlier work \citep{Sa2026} with exact gains: it fixes the sign of the average effect, while $g_i$ may be negative
on a region of positive measure. The anchor constraint, at $k$-means centres, is point-wise but only on $\A$. Point-wise
monotonicity on $\B$ implies both conditions (with $\varepsilon=0$), so the notions are nested. The sign constraint holds
everywhere:

\begin{proposition}[Sign constraints]\label{prop:sign}
If \eqref{eq:sign} holds, then $h_i\ge0$ on $\R^p$ and $p_\theta$ is $s$-monotone on $\R^p$.
\end{proposition}

This is the non-negative-weight construction \citep{ArcherWang1993,DanielsVelikova2010} for partial monotonicity: every
hidden unit must itself be monotone, with the prescribed direction, in every constrained input.

\subsection{Expressiveness}\label{sec:expressive}
That requirement costs expressiveness (\cref{fig:surfaces}). The target of \cref{fig:surfaces}, increasing in both
inputs, has an L-shaped decision boundary. Under \eqref{eq:sign} the logit is a sum of ridges increasing in both inputs, the boundary
cuts the corner (panel c), and the error is three times that of the unconstrained network, which forms the corner but
decreases in some input where data are scarce (panel b). The network of \cref{sec:cegis} forms the corner and is
certified (panel d): two of its units decrease in one input each (panel f) and together act like the term
$-|x_1-x_2|$ in the identity $\min\{x_1,x_2\}=\tfrac12(x_1+x_2-|x_1-x_2|)$. With more units the sign-constrained error stops decreasing,
whereas the certified networks follow the unconstrained ones (Supplementary \cref{S-fig:capacity}). The obstruction lies
in the parametrisation:

\begin{theorem}[Universal approximation with a certificate margin]\label{thm:ua}
Let $f:\B\to(0,1)$ be continuous and $s$-monotone. For every $\varepsilon>0$ there is an SLFN \eqref{eq:slfn} with
$\sup_\B|p_\theta-f|<\varepsilon$ and $\min_{\B}h_i>0$ for every $i\in\I$.
\end{theorem}

The proof smooths $f$ by a monotone mollification, adds a small linear term that creates a gain margin, and uses the
density of SLFNs in $C^1$ \citep{Hornik1991}. The margin also makes the certificate of \cref{sec:certificate} complete
for such networks (\cref{prop:term}).

\subsection{Counterexample-guided constraints}\label{sec:cegis}
The \emph{cegis} mode (\cref{alg:cegis}) searches the class of \cref{thm:ua} directly. At size $n$ the network is trained
without constraints and projected by SLSQP onto the anchor constraints \eqref{eq:point}. An adversarial search then
minimises each $h_i$ over $\B$ (L-BFGS-B from random points and from the training points with the smallest $h_i$, with
$\nabla_xh_i=\sum_jc_{ij}\sig''(z_j)w_j$); every point with $h_i<0$ becomes an anchor and the network is re-solved.
Anchors are inherited from size to size. After the final refit the certificate of \cref{sec:certificate} is run; its
counterexamples also become anchors (\emph{repair}). If the network is still not certified after $R_{\mathrm{rep}}$
repairs, the weights with $c_{ij}<0$ are set to zero and the network is re-solved under \eqref{eq:sign} (\emph{fallback}).

\begin{proposition}[Certified output]\label{prop:always}
The network returned by the cegis mode is $s$-monotone on $\B$: it is either certified (\cref{thm:sound}) or satisfies
\eqref{eq:sign} (\cref{prop:sign}).
\end{proposition}

\begin{algorithm*}[!tbp]
\caption{Constructive step $n$ in the cegis mode.}\label{alg:cegis}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require data $(X,y)$, signals $s$, anchors $\A$ ($k$-means centres and earlier counterexamples), initial weights
$\theta^{(0)}$ (WILCAR: previous network plus one unit; RIXM: random)
\State $\theta\gets$ L-BFGS on $J$ from $\theta^{(0)}$; \ $\theta\gets$ SLSQP on $J$ subject to \eqref{eq:point} on $\A$, from $\theta$
\For{$r=1,\dots,R_{\mathrm{cex}}$}
  \State $C\gets\{\hat x_i: i\in\I,\ h_i(\hat x_i)<0\}$, \ $\hat x_i\gets$ multi-start L-BFGS-B on $\min_{x\in\B}h_i(x)$
  \If{$C=\emptyset$} \textbf{break} \EndIf
  \State $\A\gets\A\cup C$; \ $\theta\gets$ SLSQP on the enlarged $\A$, warm-started at $\theta$
\EndFor
\State \Return $\theta$, $\A$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm*}

This is the discretisation (exchange) scheme of semi-infinite programming \citep{Hettich1993}: the infinite family of
constraints $h_i(x)\ge0$, $x\in\B$, is replaced by a finite set that grows with the most violated points.

\subsection{A sound certificate}\label{sec:certificate}
By \cref{lem:gain}, $p_\theta$ is $s$-monotone on $\B$ if and only if $\min_\B h_i\ge0$ for every $i\in\I$. A branch and
bound decides this for each $i$ (Supplementary \cref{S-alg:bb}): it bounds $h=h_i$ from below on a box; if the bound is
non-negative the whole box is proved; if $h<0$ at a point of the box, that point is a counterexample; otherwise the box
is split. Two bounds are used. On a box $Q=[l,u]$ with centre $m$ and radius $r$, each pre-activation ranges exactly over
$[\alpha_j,\beta_j]=[w_j^\top m+b_j\mp|w_j|^\top r]$; since $\sig'$ is even and unimodal, its minimum there is
$\underline{d}_j=\min\{\sig'(\alpha_j),\sig'(\beta_j)\}$ and its maximum $\overline{d}_j=\sig'(\clip(0;\alpha_j,\beta_j))$.
With an interval enclosure $[G^-_d,G^+_d]$ of $\partial h/\partial x_d$ on $Q$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lbx}
\min_{x\in Q}h(x)\ \ge\ \max\Bigl\{\sum_{c_j\ge0}c_j\underline{d}_j+\sum_{c_j<0}c_j\overline{d}_j,\ \
h(m)-\sum_{d=1}^{p}r_d\max\{|G^-_d|,|G^+_d|\}\Bigr\},
\end{equation}
the natural interval extension and the mean-value form \citep{Moore2009}; the monotonicity test of interval global
optimisation \citep{HansenWalster2004} collapses a box onto a face when $G^-_d>0$ or $G^+_d<0$. When $n<p$ the search runs
in pre-activation space, where $h=\varphi(Wx+b)$ with $\varphi(z)=\sum_jc_j\sig'(z_j)$ separable, so that its minimum on
a box $R=[\zeta^l,\zeta^u]$ is exact:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lbz}
\min_{z\in R}\varphi(z)=\sum_{c_j\ge0}c_j\min\{\sig'(\zeta^l_j),\sig'(\zeta^u_j)\}+\sum_{c_j<0}c_j\,\sig'\bigl(\clip(0;\zeta^l_j,\zeta^u_j)\bigr);
\end{equation}
boxes outside the zonotope $\{Wx+b:x\in\B\}$ are discarded by a linear program in $x$.

\begin{theorem}[Soundness]\label{thm:sound}
If the branch and bound returns \textsc{certified} for every $i\in\I$, $p_\theta$ is $s$-monotone on $\B$; a returned
counterexample $\hat x$ lies in $\B$ and satisfies $s_ig_i(\hat x)<0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proposition}[Termination]\label{prop:term}
If $\min_\B h_i\neq0$ and boxes are processed breadth-first, the input-space search terminates after finitely many boxes.
\end{proposition}

Bounding a box costs $O(np)$ operations; the number of boxes is exponential in $\min\{n,p\}$ in the worst case, but in
practice it is governed by the margin $\min_\B h_i$. Undecided cases (a minimum exactly at zero, or the box budget) are
resolved by the fallback. The certificate applies to any SLFN, so we also use it to measure how often unconstrained and
mean-constrained networks are monotone.

\subsection{Dynamic cross-validation}\label{sec:dcv}
Dynamic cross-validation (DCV) selects the number of units inside a training set: $K$ stratified folds, one model per
fold, all grown in lock-step; at size $n$ the criterion is the sum of the $K$ validation cross-entropies, and growth stops
after the patience. The final network is retrained on the whole training set with the selected size (Supplementary
\cref{S-alg:dcv}). The criterion is informative only if the $K$ curves change smoothly with $n$, which weight reuse
provides; we therefore use DCV with WILCAR, and a stratified 80/20 hold-out split with the validation MCC otherwise.

\section{Experimental setup}\label{sec:setup}

\emph{Data.} Seven synthetic $s$-monotone targets $P(Y=1\mid x)$ on $[0,1]^p$, $p\in\{2,3\}$ (Supplementary
\cref{S-tab:synthdef}): the ``and'' target of \cref{fig:surfaces}, an ``or'' (maximum), a product, a three-input
minimum, versions with a free periodic input and with a decreasing input, and a smooth ``wave'' with no min/max
structure; 2000 uniform inputs with Bernoulli labels each. The error against the known probability is measured on
$2\times10^4$ uniform points. Because the question is what each class can represent, every constructive step keeps the
best of three initialisations on these targets. The eight real datasets are listed in \cref{tab:datasets}; the signal
vectors of the five UCI datasets come from domain knowledge (Supplementary \cref{S-tab:signals}), and the last three are
the benchmarks of the monotonic-network literature, with the preprocessing and monotone features of \citet{Runje2023}.

\IfFileExists{tables/datasets.tex}{\input{tables/datasets.tex}}{}

\emph{Protocol.} Nested cross-validation \citep{VarmaSimon2006,CawleyTalbot2010}: an outer stratified 5-fold split
repeated five times (twice for Loan Defaulter); inside each outer training set, scaling, model size (or hyper-parameters)
and refit use only that set, with a hold-out split or DCV ($K=4$). All methods share the splits. For the three
benchmarks we also use the official train/test split with five seeds (\cref{sec:lit}).

\emph{Methods.} WILCAR, RIXM and ELM, unconstrained and with the four constraints of \cref{sec:gains}
($\varepsilon_m=0.05$, $\varepsilon_p=10^{-3}$, $\lambda_0=0.3$); logistic regression, free and with sign-bounded
coefficients (LR, LR-C); XGBoost \citep{ChenGuestrin2016} and LightGBM \citep{Ke2017} with monotone constraints; a
one-hidden-layer MLP \citep{Pedregosa2011}; and three networks monotone by construction --- Min-Max \citep{Sill1997},
constrained monotonic neural networks (CMNN, \citealp{Runje2023}) and Lipschitz monotonic networks (LMN,
\citealp{Nolte2023}) --- from their authors' packages. Settings and grids are in Supplementary
\cref{S-tab:settings,S-tab:hyper}; each selection loop has a budget of one hour.

\emph{Metrics.} Matthews correlation coefficient (MCC, \citealp{ChiccoJurman2020}); AUC and Brier score in the
Supplementary Material; on synthetic data, the mean absolute error against the true probability. Monotonicity: the share
of (point, constrained input) pairs with a wrong-sign gain on $10^4$ uniform points of $\B$, and the outcome of the
certificate. Methods are compared with the Friedman test, the Iman--Davenport correction and Holm's procedure
\citep{Demsar2006}, and pairwise with the Wilcoxon signed-rank test over outer folds.

\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Synthetic targets}\label{sec:synth}
\IfFileExists{tables/synthetic.tex}{\input{tables/synthetic.tex}}{}
\Cref{tab:synth} confirms \cref{sec:expressive}. The certified networks reduce the error of sign-constrained ones on every
target, by 30\% on average (0.053 against 0.076), and are certified in all 175 runs. Unconstrained networks are more
accurate (0.044) but monotone in only 35\% of the runs; the average-gain constraint changes little, because on these

the three-input minimum, whose sharp corners a sum of sigmoid ridges approximates slowly.

Min-Max by $0.019$ ($p<10^{-4}$). AUC and Brier score agree (Supplementary \cref{S-tab:main_auc,S-tab:main_brier}): mean AUC 0.877 for
certified WILCAR, unconstrained WILCAR and CMNN, 0.873 for LMN, 0.869 for Min-Max and 0.881 for the monotone tree
ensembles. Trees are ahead on ALG and HF, the networks on HD.

\Cref{tab:modes} shows what each constraint delivers. Only 35\% of the unconstrained networks are monotone, with 11\% of
wrong-sign pairs; the average-gain constraint raises this to 75\% but gives no guarantee; the anchor constraint, which
leaves the space between anchors free, to 64\%. The sign and cegis constraints are monotone in every fold, at no cost in
MCC. In cegis mode, 97.3\% of the networks were certified by the branch and bound and 2.7\% (15 of 555) returned by the
fallback. The median cost per outer fold is 22 s for certified WILCAR against 18 s unconstrained; the mean is dominated
by BCD (27 constrained inputs) and Loan Defaulter, where 22 of 740 selection loops reached the one-hour budget. On a
separate population of 168 networks, the certificate decided 96\% of the networks produced by a cegis step, whose
margins are thin by design, with a median time below a millisecond (Supplementary \cref{S-tab:certbench}).

On real data the sign and cegis modes coincide in accuracy. Selection favours small networks (median $n^\ast$ of 5 for
certified WILCAR with DCV, 1 with the hold-out split; sizes and times in Supplementary \cref{S-tab:size}), and with a
single unit the two constraints are identical; the flexibility of cegis matters when the target needs more units, as in
\cref{tab:synth}.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
The constraint families form a hierarchy: the average-gain constraint of our earlier work fixes the sign of the global
effect of each input, anchor constraints fix it at chosen points, and the sign and cegis constraints fix it everywhere.
In practice the average-gain constraint already yields monotone networks in three cases out of four, consistently with
the physically coherent models reported earlier; what it cannot provide is a guarantee. Sign constraints provide one
through the parameters, at the price shown by \cref{fig:surfaces} and \cref{tab:synth}; the cegis mode provides it
through verification and keeps the flexibility of \cref{thm:ua}. Growth helps: anchors found at size $n$ remain valid at
size $n+1$, and small networks are cheap to certify because the pre-activation search works in dimension $n$.

\emph{Limitations.} The certificate relies on separability in the pre-activation space of one hidden layer; deeper
networks would need bound propagation \citep{Gowal2019,Liu2021verif}. The guarantee holds on the training box $\B$, and
the certificate is exact up to floating-point rounding. The quadratic programs limit constrained networks to tens of
units and training sets to tens of thousands of rows.

% =====================================================================================================
\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusion}
Single-hidden-layer networks that are monotone on their domain are universal approximators of continuous partially
monotone functions, while sign-constrained ones are not. Counterexample-guided constraints, a sound certificate and a
sign-constrained fallback reach that class with constructive networks: every network returned is certified, the error
of sign-constrained networks falls by 30\% on synthetic targets, and on real data the certified networks are as accurate
as unconstrained networks and CMNN, and more accurate than Min-Max and LMN. Extending the certificate to deeper
constructive networks is a natural next step.
